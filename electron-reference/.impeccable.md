## Design Context

### Users
An Iraqi Arabic-speaking restaurant owner or accountant recording daily expenses, reviewing cash movements, and tracking chicken counts. As of the owner request on 2026-10-26, this also includes cash sales and simple finished-item inventory for the same restaurant.

### Brand Personality
Clear, calm, refined. The user wants an impressive design that still feels simple to use.

### Aesthetic Direction
User-approved calm green with a light background. Arabic right-to-left layout, warm neutral surfaces, forest green accents, legible financial values, generous spacing. Build on the current Sketch draft's expenses, settlements, and other sections.

### Design Principles
- Keep daily expense recording clear. Sales uses the owner sketch: product grid on the left, bill on the right, checkout anchored at the bottom, configurable page shortcuts above.
- Give each expense a monetary amount and optional note; retain custom category names.
- Keep chicken counts separate from financial totals.
- Show explicit cash deposits and withdrawals.
- Keep supplier invoices, reusable materials, and account history together. A bill adds debt; payments add daily expenses and adjust their funding source once.
- Use illustrative data in design examples; do not invent balance formulas while requirements remain unresolved.

### Interaction rules — owner approved, 2026-10-26
- Use a dismissible navigation pop-over and centered entry dialogs; do not reserve a permanent sidebar column. Long purchases use a larger dialog with visible supplier/date and total/save context.
- Protect dirty forms, restore the active local sales draft, preserve keyboard focus, and keep saving/error state in the toolbar.
- Use the shared operational tokens in ux.css. Desktop sales can fit the window; smaller/zoomed windows must scroll to checkout and all form actions. Compact product density keeps reference images.

### Page sizing — owner screenshot correction, 2026-10-27
- Use the available window width with consistent modest gutters (24px on desktop), shared by navigation, headers, summaries, filters and panels. Do not center pages inside a narrow maximum-width container. Keep individual controls and the order panel compact; let tables and catalog use the remaining space.
- Let page headings/actions wrap naturally and keep all actions reachable on narrow windows.

- Full-window review (2026-10-27): cap date/filter controls and the bill width; never use a horizontal flex basis as a fixed height when a page header stacks vertically.

### Content composition — owner correction, 2026-10-27
- Full-width shell does not mean every component spans the screen. Keep related labels, totals and controls together; do not distribute sparse content into equal-width columns.
- Use a primary ledger with a compact secondary column for daily expense context. Empty states should fit their content rather than occupy a large decorative panel.
